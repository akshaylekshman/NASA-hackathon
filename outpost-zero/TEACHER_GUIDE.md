# Teacher Guide — OUTPOST ZERO

**Learning goals:** identify resource dependencies; compare short-term and long-term costs; explain why energy storage and redundancy matter; distinguish simplified game units from real engineering data.

**Setup:** Open `index.html` in a current browser. Choose Cadet for guided play or Rookie for younger learners. Share a seed by adding `?seed=VALUE` to the page URL.

**15-minute session:** 2 minutes for briefing, 8 minutes to play through the 15 short turns in pairs, 3 minutes to compare outcomes, and 2 minutes for reflection. Ask students to narrate a cause-and-effect chain when a meter becomes low.

**Compare results:** Have teams use the same tier and seed, then compare actions, remaining resources, health, and the turn when their power budget became tight. Game values are abstractions, not engineering specifications.

## Discussion questions

1. Which system did you build first, and what did that delay?
2. How did storing energy help during lunar night?
3. What did the crew need that competed for the same energy?
4. Why might a backup system be worth its material cost?
5. How did shielding affect your other plans?
6. When did recycling help, and what could it not solve?
7. Which warning gave you enough time to change your plan?
8. What would you want to verify before using these game values in real engineering?
